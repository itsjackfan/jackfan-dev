## Compositional, RL agent-based modelling of dynamical systems
Agent-based models are a unique class of model that allows for hierarchical and stochastic modelling of a wide range of environments, such as flocking, epidemiology, and other relevant environments. However, many of the current state-of-the-art for agent-based models do not effectively leverage innovations in machine learning (ML) and reinforcement learning (RL) when modelling different agent behaviours. Furthermore, most agent-based models instantiate agents in an environment-agnostic manner, meaning agent behaviours are not affected by external factors from the environment. As a result, we tend to see off-policy stochasticity in the behaviours of the agents, meaning the expressiveness and interpretability of the models is limited. In order to remedy this, we propose an alternative agent-based modelling architecture that makes use of a model-based RL learning process coupled with an internal hidden state that stores agent actions, environmental stimuli, and their consequences. This allows us to define more realistic, learned behaviours for individual agents and to allow for dynamic understanding of actions and consequences within these environments. To test this hypothesis, we seek to introduce two RL-augmented ABM environments and compare them against NetLOGO and real-world data baselines. From this, we conclude that RL-augmented ABM and internal dynamics modelling for agents produces outsize returns for the accuracy and performance of general ABM systems.
