*A simple raw open letter about the continued intentions of this blog moving forward.*

I've been struggling with burnout as a result of various unhealthy attitudes towards work, relationships, and personal standards in equal parts. I've also generally been doing a lot of thinking and growing, especially over the summer given some new developments in both my personal and professional life; this has led me to realise, simply, that I do not know enough about much to be able to make satisfyingly deep comments on a large variety of topics. By extension, I feel a need to read more and be more generally aware in the world, so thus comes this blog. Though there are plenty of updates, which are all mostly outlined either in [my timeline](/work) or in continued updates to [my thought graph](/graph) or [blogposts](/blog), the biggest changes can be summed up under two large categories: communication and personal assertiveness. 

*A foreword, first, about where the blog went in the past 6 months.* Originally, this blog fizzled out because I felt a pressure to continue writing and producing at a time where I felt like I had no space to do so and had different priorities (most of my time was allocated more towards social and personal progression). There were two major cycles of thought that drove this unhealthy allocation of my time. Firstly, because I was insecure in many of the friendships and relationships I had formed, I often found myself needing the reassurance of spending ill-advised late nights playing cards with friends, thus forcing me to (a) dedicate a significant portion of my energy away from myself and towards overengineering relationships that I had already formed and (b) formulate a reputation of sorts in my friends' minds that this was the "normal" way for me to spend time, and that any divergence in this was something wrong, making it quite difficult for me to correct my course. As a result of this, through the past 6 months (and really for a significant part of my childhood and young adulthood), a significant portion of my energy that was now dedicated away from myself. Reflection has elucidated the connection that this essentially comes as a problem of both anxious attachment and needing to lower the priority I place on what other people think. 

The other cycle and cause of a lot of the burnout was that there was often a significant mismatch between the time I wanted to allocate to certain projects, learning, or technical work and the time that I actually ended up allocating to these "gratification investments". In common terms, this can be seen as a form of bad time management or procrastination, especially because a lot of both the academic and industrial work I've been taking on has been self-paced, allowing me the unfortunate luxury of pushing my own schedule until the work had piled up past the acceptable threshold of stress. As in [Tim Urban's TED Talk on procrastiation](https://www.ted.com/talks/tim_urban_inside_the_mind_of_a_master_procrastinator), this was me constantly seeking instant gratification, both in terms of the pleasure of hanging out with friends and the brief respite from the anxious thoughts I would have in my head when operating from an insecure base without reassurance of the strength of the relationship (over the summer, we had our own problems about relationship effort that will perhaps be further explored in an upcoming post.)

In addition to other issues I've often dealt with about using YouTube and social medias as a form of avoidance (hence my crusade against social media and intentionality about my attention, generally), the past 6 months has therefore been an extremely clarifying, if very challenging time for me to discover why a very large proportion of my life was characterised primarily by anxiety, compulsions (as in OCD-style), and overall stress and malcontent even during events and periods that were on paper stress-free, fulfilling, and joyful.

I've now expounded pretty significantly on this problem, and those who know me note that this is unlike me; I may think deeply about problems, but usually only to the end of finding a more succinct, nuanced solution. The natural question is then: *what is the punchline of all of this?*

To return to the two points of communication and personal assertiveness, this blog has been reactivated mainly as **a method of practicing precise, succinct communication on subjects that I truly care about and want to deep-dive in** and as **an incentive for me to actively invest in thoroughly researching, experimenting with, and formulateing clear, assertive opinions about subjects that I feel a need to be able to speak confidently in general conversation about** (the second point in particular is a more refined, mentally clear picture of what I originally started the blog for as well). Why, exactly, are both important and relevant to what I've discussed above? To justify both sides, I turn to the following heuristic of a standard conversation with someone else. I believe it is relatively defensible to conjecture that the most interesting conversations come between people that are well-read enough to be able to meaningfully participate in any conversation, and furthemore have enough substantive, well-justified opinions (bonus if in the same or proximal fields) that differ enough to build healthy, constructive dissent (iron sharpens iron). The natural corollary to this is then that you should build this "T" of knowledge for yourself wherever possible; for me, as someone who has always enjoyed reading and writing, wrapping up a complex subject in as crisp and comprehensible a vehicle as possible serves both of these purposes maximally. To conclude and tie everything together, if executed correctly, this blog serves both as a way for me to deepen my knowledge and atomize it in an interpretable, published form while also providing a vehicle to stay more intentional and eliminate the burnout/guilt from being unable to do so this past semester and summer.

As a final further general outline for the particular topics of this blog, I remain quite interested in the field of machine learning and AI research as a whole and furthermore on the philosophy and psychology of mind, amalgamating various different aspects of life together to be able to most fully understand the present moment. Both of these topics will continue to feature strongly in this blog. However, in the name of building "substantive, well-justified opinions" (in this case on what the blog should be about), I feel the need to make more clear what specifically I will be writing about. 

On the side of machine learning, my most central claim is that it is clear that existing machine learning models do not learn as efficiently as the human brain, and as a result that continual learning, efficient and legitimate recursive self-improvement (as opposed to some form of algorithmic brute-force search), model architecture design, and other core problems in machine learning will suffer slower progress. The development of efficient learning, in my opinion, rests upon the pillars of the different methods of collecting and representing the data (where I am quite bitter-lesson and naturalist-pilled, believing that preprocessing should be as minimal/maximally on-policy to humans as possible), the actual thinking method itself (where I am again bitter-lesson pilled, but in a different way where I believe overengineering features like tokens and audio codecs implies that we have made principled assumptions on a problem that can be solved in a simpler, latent-reasoning style format), and the learning methodology (whereby it is perhaps possible to design more efficient learning algorithms like [Q](https://qlabs.sh) or [Flappy](https://flappingairplanes.com)). In this vein, I plan to focus the majority of my reading and interpretation in these areas, and because the "debugging" or "error hunting" papers that I've been reading have helped most with my implementation and general intuition recently, these are likely the papers that I will target. Expect additional content on more fundamental deconstructions and in-depth debug dives as well for a more general machine learning bent. 

The philosopy and life angle is perhaps a bit less directly clear as compared to the ML topics above. I believe that the primary problem I had had previously was that a lot of the claims that I made were large and vacillatory, thus being subject to the Barnum effect where it was almost surely true but almost surely meaningless and general as well. Furthermore, many of the things I was thinking about that may have been relevant to myself were understandably potentially egocentric (NOT egotistical, note the difference), being disconnected or hard to specify against a more general populace or outside of perfect vacuum settings. Because I've found that the most lasting principles often sit on top of another piece (whether blogpost, book, treatise, etc.), I plan for this side of my thinking to be evaluated more specifically against conversations, readings, and other concrete pieces that I stitch together in order to communicate one clear idea that I'm currently focussing on.

Finally, in the vein of succinctness, I reiterate: the goal of this new blog era is to force myself to stop hand-waving whether I'm talking about ML or life and, because I am working to be secure in my opinions/knowledge, become more valuable across all conversations I have. 

To the T.
